Daily World Briefing: Friday 30 October 2026

By The Daily Globe editorial · 30 October 2026 · 4 min read

Here is where some of the world's biggest stories stand on Friday 30 October 2026. This briefing is written by The Daily Globe in our own words from reporting by UN News, the United Nations' news service, which lets its content be reused with attribution. Every item links to the original report. We are not part of the United Nations, and it does not endorse this site.

Dnipro rail station strike kills at least 13

The UN's top humanitarian official in Ukraine, Matthias Schmale, condemned a Russian drone attack on the main railway station in Dnipro, which killed at least 13 civilians and injured 10 more. Media reports cited by UN News said a jet-powered drone hit the station while a train bound for Zaporizhzhia stood at the platform, and that an 11-year-old boy was among the injured. Mr Schmale cited figures from Ukraine's railway operator showing attacks on railway facilities rising from 1,206 in 2025 to 1,700 so far this year.

Separately, he said preparations to get aid into Oleshky, a Russian-occupied town in the Kherson region, were "accelerating" while a local ceasefire holds. That ceasefire is due to end on the evening of 1 November. He said mined roads are a key reason residents are trapped, so any safe passage needs demining first.

Children killed in a drone attack on a market in Sudan

UNICEF said at least five children, three boys and two girls aged between seven and 14, were killed in a drone attack on a market in Nyala, South Darfur, on Monday. It came a week after armed drone strikes reportedly killed at least 80 people in a busy market in Abyei. UNICEF says more than 400 children were reported killed or injured in Sudan between January and September, with drone attacks causing a significant share.

Haiti's hunger emergency deepens

The UN Food and Agriculture Organization says more than 5.9 million people in Haiti face high levels of acute food insecurity, driven by gang violence and a strong El Niño that is bringing drier, hotter conditions. Inflation of about 17 per cent is also eroding what families can afford. The World Food Programme warns that some 6.35 million people could face acute hunger between March and June next year, and that Haiti's humanitarian plan is less than half funded.

Afghanistan's opium production jumps fivefold

A UN Office on Drugs and Crime survey found that the area planted with opium poppy in Afghanistan grew by 330 per cent in a year, to more than 43,000 hectares, and estimated production rose by about 400 per cent to just under 1,500 tonnes. It is the first significant increase since the Taliban banned poppy cultivation in 2022. Levels are still far below those before the ban, when about 232,000 hectares were planted. UNODC says farmers appear to be hiding small plots in cereal fields and walled compounds, and that poor farmers need alternative livelihoods.

Myanmar: a "catastrophic" human rights situation

The UN's independent expert on Myanmar, Kelley Anne Eckels-Currie, said in a new report that at least 8,384 civilians have been killed by the military since the 2021 coup, up to 15 September 2026. She said the situation worsened despite the military's "100-day peace plan", with about 753 civilians killed in that period, 89 per cent of them by the military and allied forces. She also highlighted how an illicit economy, including online scam centres, illegal mining and human trafficking, is helping to fund the conflict.

The General Assembly backs ending the US embargo on Cuba, with less support

For the 34th time, the UN General Assembly called for an end to the US economic embargo on Cuba, this time by 130 votes to 17 with 25 abstentions. Last year the vote was 165 to seven with 12 abstentions, and this is the weakest backing in 20 years. The US ambassador, Mike Waltz, said "there is no blockade" and blamed Cuba's government for the country's problems, while Cuba's foreign minister said the embargo was strangling its health system.
